# Reviewers

People who read sources in this list for a real place, and will say so with their name on it.

An entry stays `candidate` until somebody does that. This page exists so that a person wondering
*who could possibly review a Chilean procurement feed* has an answer, and so that a reviewer can
say in advance what they are not the right person for. It is not a rota and not an approval
committee — a review is one reading of one source for one territory, recorded in
[`reviews/`](reviews/) and dated.

**Anybody can review.** You do not need to be on this page, you do not need write access, and you
do not need to touch YAML: open a [source review](.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/source-review.yml) issue
and a workflow turns it into a pull request. Being listed here just means people can find you.

Never done one? [`REVIEWING.md`](REVIEWING.md) walks through a real review end to end — about twenty
minutes, and it shows what each of the five checks actually catches.

## What the columns mean

- **Territories** — the places this person can actually judge a source for. A review is always
  *for somewhere*; "global" is not a territory.
- **Will not review** — what they have said they are the wrong person for, declared in advance.
  Worth filling in honestly: sources your own organisation publishes (a review is still useful
  there, it is just `self_declared: true` and a reader should see that), a territory whose
  administrative codes you do not know, a portal in a language you cannot read, a domain outside
  your competence. An empty cell means nothing has been declared, not that somebody will review
  anything.

quietly stop. Add a row with your name, your city, and what you would rather not be asked about.

## Reviewing something the Foundation publishes

Tick **Self-declared** on the form. It is not disqualifying and the review is kept exactly like any
other — the publisher usually knows the licence and the vintage better than anyone. It is recorded
because a reading by the publisher and a reading by an outsider are different kinds of evidence,
and a person reading this list is entitled to know which one they are looking at.
